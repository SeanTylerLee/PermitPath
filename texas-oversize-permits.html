TxDMV and TxPROS

Who issues Texas oversize permits

Vehicles and loads that exceed Texas legal size or weight limits need an oversize/overweight (OS/OW) permit and route from the Texas Department of Motor Vehicles. Most applications run through the Texas Permitting and Routing Optimization System (TxPROS), the state’s online permitting and routing system.

PermitPath is not a permit office and does not sell Texas oversize permits. If you need the permit itself, apply with TxDMV in TxPROS or call the TxDMV Oversize/Overweight Permits office. Current limits, fees, and permit types change. Always confirm details on the official TxDMV pages before you move.

Once the state issues the permit, the route on that document is the legal path for the load. That is the part PermitPath is built for: getting the official TxPROS route into the cab so the truck can follow it.

The permitted path

What the Texas permit route means

A Texas oversize permit is more than a size sticker. It authorizes a specific move and includes the official permitted corridor. Bridges, lane widths, construction, and county restrictions are why that route exists. Car GPS apps optimize for cars. They do not know your Texas oversize permit.

Drivers hauling Texas oversize loads need the route the state already approved. Reading turn lists from a PDF while driving is slow and easy to misread. PermitPath imports that official path from TxPROS and keeps it on the screen with turn-by-turn guidance.
