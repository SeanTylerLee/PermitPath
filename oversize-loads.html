Oversize loads stay on the permit route

Oversize loads and overweight loads cannot treat the highway like a normal car trip. The permit sets the legal path. PermitPath — also called Permit Path and Permit Nav — navigates that path on iPhone, iPad, and Android so heavy-haul drivers can follow the route they were issued.

Basics

What counts as an oversize load

An oversize load is a vehicle or cargo that exceeds the legal size limits for normal highway travel—usually width, height, length, or a combination of those. Many heavy-haul moves also exceed legal weight. Those travel as overweight loads, or as combined oversize and overweight moves.

States require permits for those moves. The permit is the legal authority. The route on the permit is the corridor you are allowed to drive. That is why oversize load navigation is a different job from passenger-car GPS.

Legal size and weight limits differ by state and by road. Always confirm the rules for your load with the issuing agency before you roll. PermitPath helps you follow the route after the permit exists. It does not replace the permit or the issuing office.

Why the path matters

Car GPS is the wrong tool for oversize loads

A normal navigation app looks for the fastest or shortest path. Oversize loads care about clearances, bridges, lane width, construction, curfews, and the exact path the permit lists. Taking a “better” turn that is not on the permit can put the load on a road it is not authorized to use.

That is the core of oversize routing: match the truck to the permit route. PermitPath is built for that match. You build the route with every waypoint the permit needs, or, for Texas, you import the official state route and drive it.

FAQ

Oversize load questions

What is an oversize load?

An oversize load is a vehicle or cargo that exceeds the legal width, height, length, or related size limits for normal highway travel. Many moves also exceed legal weight and travel as overweight or as a combined oversize and overweight load. Those moves usually require a state permit and must stay on the permitted route.

Why can’t oversize loads use regular GPS?

Car GPS apps choose the shortest or fastest path for passenger cars. Oversize loads are limited by bridges, clearances, lane widths, construction, and the exact route printed on the permit. The legal path is the permit route. PermitPath is built to follow that path.

How does PermitPath help with oversize loads?

PermitPath lets you build a permit-approved route with unlimited waypoints and navigate it on iOS and Android. For Texas oversize loads, you can upload the permit PDF or scan the QR code and import the official TxPROS route from the State of Texas.
