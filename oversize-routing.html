Oversize routing & permit routing

For heavy-haul and oversize loads, the route on the permit is the law. This guide covers what that means and how PermitPath helps you stay on it.

What is oversize routing?

Oversize routing is planning a path for a load that exceeds legal size or weight limits. Instead of “shortest” or “fastest,” the route has to respect bridge limits, lane widths, curfews, construction, and state-by-state rules. That path is usually written on your oversize or overweight permit, and you are expected to follow it exactly.

What is permit routing?

Permit routing is the route printed on the state permit itself. States may list highways, exits, turn-by-turn directions, mile markers, or instructions like “follow I‑80 WB to Exit 123, then US‑30 WB.” When you are hauling oversize or overweight, that permit routing is your official navigation plan. Your job is to match real-world driving to that paper route.

Why it matters

Missing a turn on an oversize permit route can put you on roads that are too low, too narrow, or not legal for your weight or dimensions. That can mean:

  • Tickets and fines for being off your permit routing
  • Delays while you work with the permit office to correct the route
  • Safety risks around low bridges, tight turns, and restricted roads

How PermitPath helps

Instead of forcing a car GPS to match a paper permit, you can:

  • Pin your start, end, and all permit waypoints to match the route on the permit
  • Add unlimited waypoints so every turn or major change is captured
  • Save and edit routes when a permit is updated
  • Share routes between iOS and Android so everyone on the job is on the same path

The goal is simple: the route in the app should match the route on paper.

Best practices

  • Read the permit first and highlight the official route
  • Enter the same start, end, and major waypoints into PermitPath
  • Double-check low bridges, weight limits, and curfews before you roll
  • Keep the permit and the app in sync—edit the route if the permit changes
